# Phase A6.2 (App 0.9.0): Presence zones, the light inspector, brightness delays

Three improvements of existing functionality, all App-owned. The companion (integration 1.0.0), its protocol and
every Home Assistant entity identity are unchanged. Nothing changes on upgrade.

## 1. Zones under Presence

**Ownership.** Presence owns a zone's identity and detection (`zones_api.DETECTION_KEYS`: id, name, enabled,
detection, radar, sensors, activation, timing, unavailable, holds_room). Lighting owns how lights respond
(`LIGHTING_KEYS`: targets, priority, manual, daylight, on_below, release).

**Compatibility boundary instead of a storage split.** The zone record stays `zones.ZoneConfig` in the lighting
store with its stable id, and its occupancy stays `zone_runtime.ZoneManager` (the only zone engine). The App's
`zones_api.py` gives Presence its own commands, composed of the existing validated zone commands through the
lighting command router (so it works wherever lighting runs):

| Command | Does |
| --- | --- |
| `smart_occupancy/zones/room` | the room's zones for Presence (detection, state, history, holds) and what depends on each zone (lights responding, brightness cells) |
| `smart_occupancy/zones/set` | create a zone or change detection keys only; any lighting key is refused (`not_presence`) |
| `smart_occupancy/zones/remove` | delete; `confirm` required when lights or brightness cells depend on it |
| `smart_occupancy/zones/radar_test` | a test distance against a draft band (sends nothing) |

No data migration was needed: a later release can split the record (versioned, keeping the ids) behind the same
commands. Future consumers (climate) read `ZoneManager` states, as lighting and the room hold do.

**Saving sends nothing.** A zone rebuild caused by an edit that removes or switches off no zone runs with
`ZoneManager.quiet`;
the controller then takes the resulting winners as its baseline (`async_zones(..., "zone_config")`): no command,
no decision. Deleting a zone keeps the confirmed 0.6.0 behaviour (its lights follow the remaining rules at once)
and now also removes its brightness matrix column and delays (`LightMatrix.without_columns`), so a new zone with
the same id starts empty.

## 2. The light inspector and Identify

`light_inspector.inspect()` reads only: registry and capabilities, the room's mode, mood, occupancy, active
zones, ambient category, the item of `effective_plan()` (the plan every executor and the Supervisor use) with its
rule and the rules it superseded, override, Priority Inputs, Supervisor check, conflicts, pending delay, and the
room's decision and Supervisor logs filtered to the light (no second log). The winning rule, `last_commands` and the
reported state are separate fields; `last_external` with source `unattributed` is shown as "Home Assistant did not
say who".

`light_identify.IdentifyManager` (one controller, one executor):

1. `check()`: a controlled light (or a member of a managed group), available, not already identified; a group needs
   `whole_group`.
2. Snapshot (`state`, and while on: `brightness`, the attribute of its `color_mode`, `effect` - only what it
   reports, so nothing unsupported is sent back); journal saved with the controller state at once.
3. `turn_on` (`brightness_pct: 100` only if it dims) through `_send(purpose="identify", explicit=True)`: the fenced
   path, its own context (reports are "ours": no override), not reported to the Supervisor.
4. Hold for `IDENTIFY_SECONDS` (2.5 s, Home Assistant's timer). Meanwhile `_targets()` gives the plans the
   snapshot state, so a rule that wants something else produces a command, and `_send` sees the hold:
   `before_command()` marks the operation *superseded* and lets the command through after it ended (a global
   adjustment update waits instead). `_send_for_person` (switches) does the same; `_on_light_event` marks a
   change by a person, a switch or anything not ours as *interrupted*. The Supervisor skips the light and refuses
   corrections.
5. Restore only if nothing superseded it and the light still shows what Identify set (on, full brightness where it
   dims); a group's members get their own snapshots.
6. Journal removed. A restarted controller restores a journal entry only within `RECOVERY_SECONDS` (120 s) and if
   the light is still on at full brightness and unchanged since the hold ended; otherwise it is dropped. Nothing is
   suppressed after a restart. A companion-side restoration was considered and not added: the App-side journal
   covers an App restart and a lost lease without a protocol change, and the companion's fenced executor still
   authorises every command.

## 3. Delay On / Delay Off

Config: `LightMatrix.delays` `{column: {category: (on, off)}}`, 0-3600 s, only non-zero pairs, only on cells with a
value above 0; serialised only when set (older matrices serialise exactly as before); templates carry a room and a
zone column of delays. Lighting store 1.10 (no conversion).

Runtime: `brightness_delays.DelayTracker`, at most one pending action per target, never an executor.
`gate()` runs inside `async_apply` and `async_zones` right before they send (executing rooms only); it holds an
Off -> On switch-on of a matrix cell (its Delay On) and an On -> Off switch-off (plan `turn_off` or a zone release)
of an occupied room after the *lit cell* (the cell that last kept the light on) stopped applying (its Delay Off).
Held items become `unchanged` (`delay_on` / `delay_off`) and are not sent. When the timer fires, `async_delay_due`
plans again (fresh), passes the item through the same gate and sends only what is still due. While waiting, another
cell that still requests On keeps the original start with its own delay and value; anything that no longer requests
the transition cancels it. `_send` (any other purpose than global appearance updates and Identify) and
`_send_for_person` supersede a pending action of the lights they command; an external change, vacancy, Manual mood,
Observing and a removed matrix cancel it. Triggers `activated`, `resumed`, `reconnected` apply at once. Times are
Home Assistant UTC point-in-time timers (frozen clock in tests); a fired timer marks its action due. Pending actions
are never persisted; the lit cell is (a reference only).

## Known limitations

- Zones are still stored in the lighting configuration (behind Presence's commands); the split of the record is
  left to a later release.
- A remote that reports its own change within 20 s of an Identify command with no user in its context looks like
  our echo; the state check before restoring catches a changed brightness or a switch-off, not a change of colour
  only.
- A delay pending at a restart is dropped (never replayed); the next trigger decides afresh.
- Identify history and pending-delay history are kept in memory (decision log entries persist as before).
- The Presence/Lighting split is enforced by the Presence commands (detection keys only, confirmation to delete);
  the older `smart_occupancy/lighting/zone/set|remove` commands still accept every key (kept for compatibility;
  the App's Lighting page sends lighting keys only).
- Not verified on a production HAOS installation.

## Independent review

A separate review of arbitration, stale timers, physical command safety and persisted configuration found no
deadlock, double execution or second executor, and nine issues, fixed except the last: global adjustment updates
superseded Identify (they now wait for the restore); Identify sent brightness to Dumb Lights (now on/off only);
a stale lit cell could hold a light after its zone was deleted (delays are looked up again); switching an occupied
zone off kept its lights (it now ends like a deletion); a member's managed group was not held during Identify
(held and planned as before, its reports expected); a store write error could leak a hold (inside the guarded
block now); a colour change by a remote was restored over (checked now); a fired Delay On could skip a longer delay
of a newly winning cell (rescheduled); the split of zone keys is enforced only by the Presence commands
(documented above).
